How to Play

The enemy's fourth boat has a computer that can crack normal text, block code, and binary instantly — but it can't intercept silent signals sent through a user interface. So you'll send your agent instructions as a nibble: 4 bits (ones and zeros), one bit for each job.

BitMeaning01
1stMoveStay in placeMove forward 1 square
2ndTurn rightDon't turn rightTurn 90° right
3rdTurn leftDon't turn leftTurn 90° left
4thSonar pingNo pingPing for nearby boats

Examples: 1001 = move forward, then ping. 1100 = move forward, then turn right.

A ping sends back its own nibble:

ResponseMeaning
0000Boat is not adjacent
0001Boat is in a square adjacent to the agent
0010Boat is directly in front of the agent
1000Boat is in the agent's square — found!

Watch the arrow on the map below — it shows exactly where your agent is and which way it's facing, and it updates after every command. The enemy boat stays hidden until you find it, so use your ping responses (and your own marks on the map) to figure out where it's hiding.

Careful: if a move would push the agent off the edge of the map, or you try to turn both directions at once, the whole command is rejected and nothing happens.
